Description in English

The cat in ancient Egypt was one of the animals most closely associated with the daily life and religious beliefs of the ancient Egyptians. The cat was not merely a domestic animal; it was associated with the home, protection, and fertility, and held an important religious status. Cats appeared in Egyptian art from early periods, and their importance increased during the Late Period, particularly with the spread of the worship of the goddess Bastet. She was often depicted as a woman with the head of a cat or as a complete cat. Bastet was associated with protection, motherhood, fertility, and joy. Large numbers of cat mummies have also been discovered, many of which were offered as votive offerings in temples, particularly in areas such as Bubastis and Saqqara. These mummies reveal the widespread worship of cats in ancient Egypt and the important role animals played in ancient Egyptian rituals and beliefs. Through this artifact, visitors can discover an important aspect of ancient Egyptian civilization, where daily life, religion, and art were closely connected. The cat was transformed from an animal that lived alongside humans into a sacred symbol associated with protection and the gods.
